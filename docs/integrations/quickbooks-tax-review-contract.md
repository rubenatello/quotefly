# QuickBooks tax review contract

The internal tax review contract and preparatory operation ledger are prerequisites for taxable invoice support. The current source candidate assembles signed reviews using an internal provider-read path, but exposes no tax-review route or provider writer. The existing invoice publisher still blocks positive QuoteFly tax and explicitly publishes non-taxable lines.

`src/services/quickbooks-tax-review-contract.ts` validates a deliberately supplied snapshot of the invoice, customer, mappings, provider facts, and distinct origin/destination addresses. The initial address scope is the 50 US states and DC, with USD amounts. Territories, military addresses, exemptions, and unknown customer tax status need separate provider evidence before support is expanded.

The contract binds tenant, quote, invoice/version, customer, connection/realm/generation/environment, transaction date, mapping review versions/timestamps, customer and item provider versions, item tax-classification fingerprints, preference/company fingerprints, addresses, ordered lines, explicit TAXABLE/NON_TAXABLE intent, and exact invoice totals. A caller must obtain these facts under live tenant and manager authorization. Caller-provided fingerprints do not prove trusted origin or freshness.

Money enters as bounded decimal strings matching the existing Decimal(10,2) fields. Validation uses integer cents, including non-negative half-up rounding of quantity times unit price to the line amount. Inconsistent lines are rejected instead of silently changing their quantity or price. This local rounding rule still requires real QuickBooks parity evidence. The provider payload uses bounded JSON numbers only after validation.

The preparatory Estimate payload contains explicit customer/item references, USD, transaction date, ShipFromAddr, ShipAddr, and TAX/NON line codes. It omits manually supplied tax totals, rates, agencies, and internal costs. QuickBooks must calculate the actual tax. Intuit documents transaction address behavior and automatic tax requirements in its [Invoice API reference](https://developer.intuit.com/app/developer/qbo/docs/api/accounting/most-commonly-used/invoice).

On September 23, the rendered [Estimate API reference](https://developer.intuit.com/app/developer/qbo/docs/api/accounting/all-entities/estimate) also documented `ShipFromAddr` for automatic tax, tied that behavior to `Preferences.TaxPrefs.PartnerTaxEnabled`, and described QuickBooks-calculated transaction tax and totals. This supports investigating the Estimate approach; it does not prove the connected sandbox's setup or positive-tax and linked-Invoice parity. The current `UsingSalesTax` prerequisite alone must not be treated as proof of automated-tax capability. The reference's older linked automated-tax blog returned a missing-page response, so it supplies no additional evidence.

Canonical source/payload hashes and an HMAC bind the complete review. Returned data is deeply frozen. Source JSON, payloads, hashes, and bindings are internal business data and must not be logged as content-free operational evidence. Only the separate assessment and fixed failure codes are suitable for that purpose. Every assessment retains `taxCalculationProven: false`, `estimateInvoiceParityProven: false`, and `publishingAuthorized: false`.

`src/services/quickbooks-tax-estimate-ledger.ts` persists signed reviews under live owner/admin authorization. The review key is derived from the current token-encryption key with a distinct HKDF purpose; the previous configured key supports an in-flight review during rotation. The ledger records immutable source/payload snapshots, separate stable Estimate and Invoice request IDs, and a single active revision per tenant/invoice. Composite foreign keys, forced tenant RLS, column-specific runtime grants, and database triggers protect identity and retained evidence.

Persistence and claim recheck the connection generation, realm binding, setup confirmation, invoice/version/lines, source lifecycle, and reviewed mappings. Only a draft invoice with pending payment, no amount paid, and its full total outstanding is eligible. The exact customer, job, quote, and invoice lifecycle rows remain locked through validation and persistence. Provider observations must be no older than five minutes, with at most five seconds of future clock skew. The signer is an internal trusted-reader boundary: no public route may sign client-supplied provider facts. Observation timestamps and fingerprints alone do not establish actual provider provenance.

Contract version two also binds the exact immutable context ID, revision, input hash, confirmer and confirmation time, source job, connection generation and stripped customer fingerprint. The assembler accepts only a local invoice ID and expected context revision. It captures the source under the publication lock, performs bounded provider reads outside the transaction, then rechecks authorization, setup, context and every source binding before signing and persistence. Raw snapshot preparation/persistence helpers are private.

Before claim, the persisted key ID must match the configured current or previous purpose-derived key. The service recomputes the signed review, compares the binding digest in constant time, and checks both the canonical stored Estimate snapshot and its hash against the regenerated payload before recording any attempt. An unchanged unattempted review can be replaced under the current key after rotation; attempted evidence cannot be replaced. Legacy version-one rows remain readable with null context bindings and cannot authorize a new claim. A populated local migration rehearsal preserved four legacy rows and their original evidence byte-for-byte while adding the new constraints; hosted rollout and provider behavior remain separate evidence.

The tax ledger and existing direct invoice publisher share a tenant-first, per-invoice lock. Any existing direct invoice operation or retained legacy provider Invoice ID blocks the tax path, including archived or soft-deleted evidence. Conversely, an active tax review or any attempted tax operation blocks direct invoice publishing. Superseded, never-attempted reviews release that block. This interlock prevents either workflow from ignoring the other's durable accounting evidence.

A claim lasts two minutes and records a separate, write-once hash of the original attempt token. An additive follow-up migration makes the original attempt timestamp write-once as well, including for database-owner updates. Expiry moves the operation to reconciliation-required; it never authorizes another create. Only unattempted reviews can be superseded through this service. A matching late response may retain its provider Estimate ID after lease expiry, disconnection, or supersession, without authorizing a new provider request. Conflicting identities return a fixed error; when PostgreSQL omits the unique-constraint target, the service confirms the exact conflicting identity in a new tenant-scoped transaction after rollback. No helper currently transitions an operation to canonical or changes financial totals.

There is intentionally no provider adapter or linked Invoice builder yet. The next implementation needs trusted provider observations, dispatch-time authorization and source revalidation, immediate persistence of returned provider IDs, and reconciliation of ambiguous writes. Before publishing, canonical Estimate and full linked Invoice reads must establish exact customer, address, line, subtotal, tax, total, and link parity. A positive-tax sandbox fixture must produce positive provider-calculated tax; an exempt or zero-tax response cannot substitute for it.

The existing Job and branding fields cannot alone assemble an authoritative tax review: `Job.serviceAddressSnapshot` is free-form and the branding address has no country or confirmed sale/ship-from meaning. The new internal context stores explicitly confirmed structured origin/destination and transaction date separately. Do not infer jurisdiction from free-form text or silently substitute invoice creation time.

`src/services/quickbooks-tax-context.ts` implements an internal append-only invoice context with child line records. It binds manager-confirmed structured addresses, an explicit calendar date, the invoice version and complete ordered lines, connection generation, exact reviewed customer/item mappings, and each line's tax intent. The transaction date uses PostgreSQL DATE; confirmation and lifecycle instants remain UTC timestamps. One active revision, tenant-composite relationships, forced RLS, immutable evidence, a sealed child count and no runtime deletion preserve provenance. Historical line IDs are snapshots rather than live foreign keys; the complete locked source is rechecked before use. Accepted request keys remain bound to their original source. An unchanged submission using a new key is rejected instead of acknowledging a key that was never persisted.

Any active, unsuperseded context containing TAXABLE intent blocks the existing direct non-tax preview, claim and final provider-create fence under the shared publication lock, even if that context has become stale. Explicit replacement with fully confirmed NON_TAXABLE lines can release that block when no competing or attempted operation exists. Late provider identity retention remains available. A provider default or zero quoted tax cannot substitute for contractor confirmation.

Commit `cef5427` implements the manager-only context endpoint. The next InvoicePanel form collects explicit decisions, with existing structured profile values as editable suggestions only. Its public request cannot supply authoritative prices, provider identities or mapping versions. The internal assembler binds the active context to the tax ledger, but does not calculate tax or authorize publishing. Any future dispatcher must independently revalidate the complete source at its final provider-write boundary.

The capture endpoint must also detect changes between loading and confirming the form. Invoice version alone does not detect a changed customer/item mapping or connection. A server-issued, purpose-bound review token should bind the complete current source and be compared again under the publication lock. The browser submits explicit address/date/line-intent decisions plus its expected context revision and command key; the server reloads all financial and provider bindings. A stale form must require a fresh review rather than silently confirming newer mappings. Manager-private form responses require no-store caching and must not expose credentials or provider response bodies.

### Implemented capture API contract

- Add authenticated owner/admin GET and POST at `/integrations/quickbooks/invoices/:invoiceId/tax-context`, using the existing accounting-workflow availability guard. Both success and error responses are private/no-store. Return fixed customer-safe errors; never attach Zod input, addresses, tokens or provider payloads to diagnostics.
- GET reloads the current tenant-scoped invoice, accepted quote, active job/customer, confirmed connection generation and reviewed customer/item maps under the shared publication lock. Return display prices and line IDs, current context assessment/revision, editable prior decisions if present, and a short-lived source token. First-time line intent and transaction date remain unset; profile address suggestions must not count as confirmation.
- The purpose-derived source token authenticates tenant, live actor/auth version, invoice, expected context revision, expiry and a canonical hash of the complete current financial/provider binding. It carries no addresses, provider identifiers or credentials. Use a distinct key purpose from the signed Estimate review, strict token parsing, constant-time signature checks, and only explicitly configured current/previous keys. A proposed fifteen-minute lifetime limits stale forms; expiry requires a reload.
- POST accepts only transaction date, structured origin/destination, ordered local invoice-line IDs with explicit tax intent, expected context revision, UUID command key and the source token. Reject extra trusted-source fields. Reload bindings and compare the authenticated source hash inside the same transaction and lock used to append the context; do not verify in one transaction and persist in another. Reuse existing replay/conflict semantics, including unchanged-command handling. Bind the expected revision separately so an exact retry can still resolve its already-accepted command without treating a newer context as permission to overwrite it.
- Refactor the internal source loader to derive invoice prices and uniquely reviewed maps itself; both the form adapter and existing stored-context assessment must use the same validation. Keep provider I/O outside this capture path. Capturing intent must not create an Estimate, calculate tax, change invoice totals or authorize publishing.
- Required tests include unauthenticated/staff/revoked-manager/cross-tenant denial; extra-field and malformed-token rejection; expiry/key rotation; changed prices, mappings and connection generation after GET; competing invoice operation; context revision races; exact replay and command conflict; complete ordered line intent; and no provider calls, financial mutation or sensitive error output.

Backend design review further requires a purpose-keyed binding MAC rather than exposing a raw source hash. Bind the live tenant membership and auth version, complete ordered priced lines, source lifecycle, confirmed setup/checklist/scopes/realm binding, and mapping review identities/timestamps. Issue with the current key only; verify an exact key ID against current/previous keys, fixed purpose/version, bounded issued/expiry timestamps and strict encoding. The token supplements existing cookie/CSRF checks. Keep GET locks short and provider-free; show mapping labels/status without provider IDs. POST replay must use canonical semantic decisions and source binding rather than raw token bytes, so harmless token reissuance/rotation does not conflict. Define expired accepted-replay behavior explicitly and test it, together with cross-actor token copying, future issuance, excessive lifetime, removed keys, setup/scope drift and the 500-line bound. Preserve non-enumerating cross-tenant errors and no-store headers even on framework validation failures.

`src/services/quickbooks-tax-provider-facts.ts` is an internal GET-only reader for strict Customer/Item tax projections and CompanyInfo/Preferences through the established credential wrapper, outside database transactions. The internal assembler consumes it; there is no public reader route. Customer exemption or missing tax status remains unsupported; only active Service items with explicit tax status matching the supplied line intent qualify. Keyed fingerprints bind the stripped provider facts without returning customer contact data, exemption reasons, item prices, or account references. They remain internal business data and must not be logged.

The reader checks the exact connection, realm binding, environment, setup confirmation, scopes, and connection generation before and after provider reads. It accepts at most 500 lines and 25 distinct items, deduplicates item reads, and limits parallel reads to four. Resource GETs share a 20-second deadline, including a possible credential-refresh replay, with no transport read retries. Credential acquisition and refresh retain the existing wrapper's configured timeouts and durable fences; the resource deadline is not an end-to-end request deadline. The wrapper's setup-confirmation fence remains mandatory; the OAuth-only staging connection cannot bypass it for a diagnostic.

The assembler revalidates live manager authorization, all source versions, and competing operations after the provider reads, then signs and persists internally. Reader and assembler success still report tax calculation and publishing authorization as false. None of these prerequisites or mock tests substitute for actual sandbox tax behavior.

Intuit also publishes a separate Sales Tax GraphQL calculator. Its [official Java sample](https://github.com/IntuitDeveloper/SampleApp-SalesTax-Java) requires the additional `indirect-tax.tax-calculation.quickbooks` scope. An [Intuit developer article](https://medium.com/intuitdev/unlocking-project-tracking-with-the-projects-api-c0812fafa2dc) identifies Sales Tax among Silver-or-higher Premium APIs, with published Silver pricing of US$300 per month in its [partner program guide](https://static.developer.intuit.com/resources/Intuit_App_Partner_Program_Guide.pdf). QuoteFly's entitlement, sandbox behavior, calculation persistence semantics, and parity with Accounting API invoices have not been established. Do not silently add the scope, purchase access, or treat the sample as a no-side-effect preview guarantee. Revisit this option before choosing the final provider adapter. The present Estimate approach likewise needs explicit current sandbox proof of positive tax calculation and linked Invoice parity before its writer is implemented or enabled.

Focused contract checks run with `tsx --test tests/unit/quickbooks-tax-review-contract.test.ts` and are included in `npm run test:unit`. The schema and service suites in `tests/integration/quickbooks-tax-estimate-ledger-*.test.ts` require the migrated isolated test database and exercise the least-privileged runtime role. Passing these local checks does not establish provider compatibility, customer-facing tax support, or production release readiness.
